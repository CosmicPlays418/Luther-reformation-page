Martin Luther and His Reform

Born November 10, 1483 – Eisleben, Saxony
Died February 18, 1546 – Eisleben, Saxony

Married to Katharina von Bora from June 13, 1525 until his death.

His influence on the Protestant Reformation can be shown through three key events: The posting of the 95 Theses, the Diet of Worms, and the translation of the Bible into German.

Key Events of the Reformation

The Posting of the 95 Theses (1517)

On October 31, Luther posted his 95 Theses on the door of a church in Wittenberg. These theses challenged the sale of indulgences and the authority of the Pope. This moment is considered the beginning of the Protestant Reformation.

The Diet of Worms (1521)

At the Diet of Worms, Luther was ordered to recant his teachings before Emperor Charles V. He refused, which resulted in his excommunication and strengthened the divide between Protestant reformers and the Catholic Church.

The German Bible Translation (1522–1534)

While hiding for protection, Luther translated the New Testament into German. This allowed common people to read the Bible themselves and supported his belief that Scripture is the highest authority in Christianity.

Major Disagreements

Sale of Indulgences

Luther strongly opposed the Church selling indulgences as a way to reduce punishment in purgatory. He believed forgiveness could not be purchased and that salvation comes through faith and God’s grace.

“The Pope has no power to remit sins except by declaring and showing that they are remitted by God’s grace.” (Luther, 1517)

Authority of Scripture vs Church Tradition

Luther believed the Bible alone should guide Christian beliefs and practices. He argued that Church traditions should not override Scripture.

“It is neither right nor safe to depart from Scripture in matters of faith.” (Luther, 1520)

The Sacrament of Communion

Luther rejected the Catholic doctrine of transubstantiation. Instead, he believed Christ is truly present during communion but that the bread and wine remain physically unchanged.

Similarities Between Luther and Modern Lutheran Churches

Salvation Through Faith Alone

Luther taught that salvation comes through faith alone, known as “sola fide.”

“The just shall live by faith.” – Romans 1:17

Modern Lutheran churches continue emphasizing God’s grace and faith in Jesus Christ for salvation.

The Bible as the Highest Authority

Luther believed Scripture was the ultimate authority, called “sola scriptura.”

“A simple layman armed with Scripture is greater than the mightiest pope without it.”

Lutheran worship services today include Bible readings and sermons based on Scripture.

The Priesthood of All Believers

Luther believed all Christians could have a direct relationship with God.

“All Christians are truly of the spiritual estate.”

Members of Lutheran churches today actively participate in worship, Bible study, and church activities.
