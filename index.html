Martin Luther and His Reform

Born November 10, 1483 – Eisleben, Saxony
Died February 18, 1546 – Eisleben, Saxony

Married to Katharina von Bora from June 13, 1525 until his death.

His influence on the Protestant Reformation can be shown through three important events: The posting of the Theses, the Diet of Worms, and the translation of the Bible into German.

Key Events

The Posting of the 95 Theses (1517)

On October 31, Luther posted his 95 Theses on the door of a church in Wittenberg. These theses challenged the sale of indulgences and papal authority. This event is widely seen as the start of the Protestant Reformation.

The Diet of Worms (1521)

At this meeting Luther was ordered to revoke his teachings before Emperor Charles V. He refused, and the Church excommunicated him, deepening the divide between Catholic and Protestant groups.

The German Bible Translation (1522–1534)

While in hiding, Luther translated the New Testament into German. This allowed ordinary people to read the Bible and supported his belief that Scripture should be the highest authority.

Disagreements with the Catholic Church

Sale of Indulgences

Martin Luther strongly opposed the Church selling indulgences, which were believed to reduce time in purgatory. He believed forgiveness could not be bought and that salvation comes through God’s grace and faith.

“The Pope has no power to remit sins except by declaring and showing that they are remitted by God’s grace.” (Luther, 1517)

Authority of Scripture vs Church Tradition

Luther believed the Bible alone should guide Christian belief and practice. He argued that many Church teachings were not supported by Scripture.

“It is neither right nor safe to depart from Scripture in matters of faith.” (Luther, 1520)

The Sacrament of Communion

Luther rejected the Catholic doctrine of transubstantiation. Instead, he believed Christ is truly present during communion but that the bread and wine remain physically unchanged.

Similarities Between Luther and Modern Lutheran Churches

Salvation Through Faith Alone

Martin Luther taught that salvation comes through faith alone (sola fide).

“The just shall live by faith.” – Romans 1:17

Lutheran churches today continue to emphasize faith and God’s grace for salvation.

The Bible as the Highest Authority

Luther believed Scripture was the ultimate authority (sola scriptura).

“A simple layman armed with Scripture is greater than the mightiest pope without it.”

Modern Lutheran worship continues to focus heavily on Bible readings and sermons.

The Priesthood of All Believers

Luther believed all Christians can have a direct relationship with God.

“All Christians are truly of the spiritual estate.”

Church members today actively participate in worship, Bible study, and community activities.
